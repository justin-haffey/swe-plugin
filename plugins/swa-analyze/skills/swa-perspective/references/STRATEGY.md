# Multi-Perspective Analysis

## Core question

Change the perceptual frame deliberately and compare what each view reveals or obscures about the architecture.

## Analyze

- Establish the dominant current view and the decisions it makes easy or difficult to see.
- Inspect the target as a part, a whole, a process over time, a purpose-serving capability, and from the perspectives of relevant users, operators, maintainers, security owners, and dependent systems.
- Note responsibilities, boundaries, feedback, failure modes, costs, and qualities that appear only in certain views.
- Find gestalt shifts where the same elements support a materially different organization or ownership model.
- Synthesize a design direction that remains coherent across the perspectives that matter.

## Recommendation test

A strong recommendation names the perspective shift, the previously hidden consequence, and the architectural change needed to serve the combined view without conflating incompatible concerns.

## Avoid

Do not enumerate stakeholders mechanically, create diagrams as a substitute for analysis, or treat every perspective as equally authoritative.
