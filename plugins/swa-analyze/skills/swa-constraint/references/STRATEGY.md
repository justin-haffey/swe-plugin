# Constraint Reframing

## Core question

Distinguish hard constraints from assumed ones so the architecture can be evaluated against a less restricted ideal and then made practical.

## Analyze

- Inventory technical, contractual, regulatory, operational, compatibility, organizational, budget, and time constraints with their evidence and owner.
- Classify each as hard, currently hard, policy-selected, local, historical, assumed, or unknown.
- Temporarily remove one negotiable constraint at a time and derive the resulting ideal responsibility, boundary, state, and interaction model.
- Reintroduce verified constraints selectively, noting which architectural benefits survive and which do not.
- Identify negotiation, experiment, migration, or authority needed to relax the highest-cost soft constraint.

## Recommendation test

A strong recommendation identifies a constraint owner and evidence, preserves truly hard limits, and shows a practical path from the unconstrained insight to a feasible design.

## Avoid

Do not recommend violating law, security, accepted contracts, or explicit authority; analysis of a removed constraint is not permission to bypass it.
